Custom dashboards draw their content from a library of 40 widgets in six categories. This page lists every widget, what it displays, when to use it, and how to read it.

You can mix categories on one dashboard; [Custom dashboards](/suite/reports/custom-dashboards/) has the steps.

## Executive insights and release readiness

These widgets answer whether the build is shippable.

| Widget | What it shows | When to use it | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **All Statuses Summary** | Summary of test run counts across all result statuses: **Passed**, **Failed**, Error, Stopped, Cancelled | Get an instant overview of test execution health at a glance | High Passed count with low Failed count indicates good health. High Error count suggests infrastructure issues |
| **Pass Fail Trends** | Historical volume of passed versus failed test runs over time | Track quality trends and detect regressions during release cycles | Rising failed tests indicate quality decline. An upward trend shows improving quality. A flat pattern indicates stability |
| **Latest Runs** | Most recent executions with build numbers, timestamps, and status, updating in real time | Monitor ongoing execution and verify CI/CD pipeline activity | Frequent recent entries show active automation. Gaps in timestamps may indicate pipeline delays or infrastructure issues |
| **Consolidated Project Health** | Aggregate pass, fail, error, stopped, and cancelled counts across all projects | Compare test health across your entire product portfolio in one view | A high pass percentage indicates overall portfolio health. Identify which projects have high failure rates for targeted improvement |
| **Total Test Plan Runs** | Cumulative count of all test plan executions in the date range, as a large KPI tile | Measure testing volume and track execution frequency over time | Higher numbers show greater coverage. Compare across periods to see whether testing intensity is increasing |
| **Test Plan Execution Analytics** | Per-plan success rate, average duration, last run, and total executions, as a sortable table | Identify slow test plans and understand which plans run reliably | Low success rates indicate flakiness. High durations reveal performance bottlenecks. Sort by duration to find the slowest plans |
| **Pass Fail Percent Trend** | Success rate percentage over rolling intervals, as (successful runs ÷ total runs) × 100 | Monitor whether suite health is improving, stable, or declining | An upward line shows improvement. A downward line indicates regression. A flat line shows a consistent maturity level |
| **Project Level Stats** | Pass and fail counts with success rate, aggregated by project | Quickly compare test health across different projects | Each project card shows overall health. Compare across cards to identify problem areas needing attention |

## Reliability and stability

These widgets separate real defects from flaky tests and infrastructure noise.

| Widget | What it shows | When to use it | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Test Health Snapshot** | Suite classification as Consistent, Flaky, or Unexecuted from recent run history, as a donut chart | Quickly assess overall suite stability at a glance | A high Consistent percentage, ideally above 80%, shows a healthy suite. A large Flaky share signals stability work. High Unexecuted may indicate coverage gaps |
| **Flaky Test List** | Tests that flip between pass and fail without code changes, ranked by Flake Score | Target high-impact flaky tests for stabilisation and root cause analysis | Tests at the top are most problematic. High flake scores indicate timing or resource-dependent issues. Start with the highest scores |
| **Top Failing Tests By User** | Most frequently failing tests, segmented by the user who triggered the run | Identify which tests fail most and spot patterns by execution source | Failures consistent across users indicate real product defects. Failures under one user may indicate environment or data issues |
| **Top Failing Tests By Day** | Most frequently failing tests, segmented by day of occurrence | Identify day-specific failure patterns | Failures concentrated on certain days may indicate scheduled maintenance, batch jobs, or data refresh cycles affecting tests |
| **Error Statistics** | Executions counted by error type: Idle Timeout, Network Error, Session Timeout, Device Not Available, and others | Spot systemic infrastructure or environment issues causing failures | High Device Not Available suggests saturation. Network Errors indicate connectivity problems. Coordinate with infrastructure teams |
| **Top Error Types** | Proportional breakdown of error type distribution | Understand the dominant failure mode and allocate investigation effort | The largest slice shows the most frequent error. If one type dominates, focus there. Similar sizes suggest distributed issues |
| **Time To Fix Test KPI** | Average duration from a test's first failure to its successful resolution, in seconds | Measure how efficiently your team diagnoses and fixes failures | Lower times indicate fast response. High times suggest slow root cause identification. Track the trend to measure improvement |
| **Stability Ratios** | Success rate grouped by attempt count (1, 2, 3, 4+), as (successful runs ÷ total runs) × 100 | Understand how often tests pass first time versus needing retries | High first-attempt success is ideal. If success improves on later attempts, tests are flaky. Consistent success shows robust tests |

## Test design and team productivity

These widgets track who is building and maintaining the suite.

| Widget | What it shows | When to use it | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tests Authored Over Time** | New test cases created in each interval, as a column chart | Monitor creation pace and ensure healthy coverage growth | Rising columns show increasing creation. Flat or declining columns may indicate saturation or resource constraints |
| **New Tests Per User** | New test cases per team member with step count, as a leaderboard or bar chart | Recognise high contributors and identify participation gaps | High contributors drive coverage. Use it to celebrate performance. Low contributors may need support or training |
| **Fixed Failed Tests Per User** | Previously failing tests successfully fixed by each user | Credit maintenance work and identify quality improvement drivers | High fixers are maintaining test health. If fixes are low team-wide, flaky tests may be accumulating |
| **Fixed Failed Tests KPI** | Total count of previously failing test cases now fixed | Measure overall stabilisation progress | Rising numbers indicate successful stabilisation. Compare periods to track maintenance effectiveness |
| **Tests Authored Vs Executed** | Tests created against tests actually run, as a dual-axis bar and line chart | Detect inactive test cases and gaps between authoring and execution | If authored far exceeds executed, inactive tests exist. A narrow gap shows healthy execution. A large gap signals stale tests |
| **Stale Assets By Category** | Test cases and test plans grouped by how long they have been inactive | Identify orphaned tests and decide whether to maintain or archive | Large stale percentages indicate unused tests. Review why they stopped executing, and delete what you do not need |
| **Distribution By Priority / Status / Type** | Test cases broken down by priority level, current status, or test type | Understand portfolio composition and ensure balanced coverage | Even distribution shows balanced coverage. Skew may indicate concentration. Adjust composition to match your risk profile |
| **Authored By App Type** | Test cases authored per application type: Web, Mobile, API, SAP, and others | Verify balanced coverage across your platform portfolio | Similar bar heights show balanced effort. Large imbalances indicate heavy investment in some platforms and gaps in others |
| **Test Cases Submitted/Assigned For Review** | Cases you created awaiting review, and cases assigned to you pending yours | Track review bottlenecks and manage approval workflows | High pending counts indicate a review backlog. Low counts show smooth approval. Monitor to keep review cycles timely |

## AI intelligence and agentic ROI

These widgets quantify what the AI layer is doing for you.

| Widget | What it shows | When to use it | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Vs Manual Composition** | Ratio of steps created by AI against manual recording or typing, as a 100% stacked bar | Quantify AI adoption and measure the shift from manual authoring | A growing AI percentage shows adoption momentum. Compare month over month to see adoption velocity |
| **Auto Healing Trend** | Failed steps against auto-healed steps over time | Show the saving from AI-driven locator and image updates | High healing numbers show significant saving, since each healed test is manual effort avoided. A rising trend justifies continued investment |
| **NLP Usage Ranked List** | Most frequently used natural language templates in step creation | Understand which capabilities teams lean on and where training is needed | Top prompts show popular features. Low-usage prompts identify underused capabilities worth training on |

## Infrastructure and performance

These widgets show whether your execution capacity fits your demand.

| Widget | What it shows | When to use it | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Concurrency Utilization Sessions** | Concurrent parallel sessions in use against the configured limit, as an overlapping area chart | Identify whether concurrency is underused or bottlenecked | The gap between used and limit shows unused capacity. Sitting near the limit suggests saturation. Plan expansion from peak usage |
| **Execution Duration Trend** | Average, median, and p95 execution duration over time, with trend lines | Detect slow runs and performance regressions | A rising trend indicates slowing execution. Sudden spikes signal a regression. Investigate prolonged runs to find bottlenecks |
| **Environment Coverage** | Run distribution by browser, OS version, and device model, as a tree map | Ensure balanced testing across supported platforms and find coverage gaps | Balanced boxes show even coverage. Dominance by one environment may leave others undertested. Match coverage to your customers |
| **App Coverage By Type** | Test runs per application type, as a donut chart | Verify effort is proportional across your app portfolio | Similar slices show balanced effort. A large imbalance means some apps get less testing. Adjust to match risk profiles |

## Accessibility

These widgets track WCAG conformance and the work still outstanding.

| Widget | What it shows | When to use it | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Compliance Score** | Overall compliance score for the selected standard, with conformance by level A, AA, and AAA | Get a quick read on how close the application is to full compliance before a release or audit | A high overall score with strong AA coverage indicates good posture. Low A-level scores suggest critical gaps that block basic requirements |
| **Issues by Severity** | Open issues by severity: Critical, Serious, Moderate, Minor, and Success | Prioritise the remediation backlog by where the severest issues sit | A large Critical or Serious slice needs immediate attention. Moderate and Minor can be planned into sprints. Success items confirm passing checks |
| **Issues by WCAG Criterion** | The top 10 WCAG criteria ranked by issue count, as a horizontal bar chart | Identify which rules the application fails most, so remediation lands where it counts | The longest bars are the highest-volume failure points. Addressing the top two or three cuts the overall issue count fastest |
| **Trend Over Time** | Compliance score across test runs or dates, as a line chart | Measure whether accessibility is improving or regressing across releases | An upward trend confirms remediation is working. A flat line means no progress. A downward trend means new issues outpace fixes |
| **Top Issues** | The most impactful open issues, with WCAG criterion, severity, pages affected, and an AI badge where Atto has a fix | Identify which issues affect the most pages and where AI-assisted remediation exists | Issues affecting many pages come first, since fixing them cuts the count most efficiently. Items with an AI badge resolve faster using Atto's suggestion |
| **Page-Level Health** | Per-URL accessibility score with a severity badge breakdown for each scanned page | Identify which pages need the most attention and track them over time | Pages with low scores or many Critical badges are the highest priority. Compare across releases to confirm improvement |
| **VPAT Readiness** | Share of WCAG criteria that have both a rule mapping and test results. Click the widget to open the VPAT export dialog | Understand how much of the criteria surface is covered before a VPAT audit or a customer request | A high percentage means most criteria are tested and documented. Gaps mean no rule coverage or no results — close these before submitting |
| **Remediation Progress** | Issues found against issues fixed, grouped by week, as a stacked bar chart | Track whether the team resolves issues faster than new ones arrive | Weeks where fixed exceeds found are net progress. The reverse is accumulating debt. A converging trend confirms the backlog is shrinking |
